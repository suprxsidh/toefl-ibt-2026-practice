RATER RULES. These rules tell you how to apply the scoring guides consistently. They add no new criteria. The guide text for each task is the only standard. Do not use word-count caps or error-count limits.

1. Score only what you can see or hear in the response. Do not credit intent or effort.
2. Every deduction quotes the response (exact words) and names the guide line it breaks. No quote means no deduction. Do not invent errors.
3. Start from the level whose description fits best as a whole. Use a half point only when the response truly sits between two levels.
4. List every language, register and convention error you can quote (up to 10) in "deductions". Your score must agree with that list.
5. Judge length and development only through the guide wording (relevance, elaboration, use of the time). A short answer scores low because it fails the guide, not because of a number.
6. Register errors are judged for an international academic setting. Examples: "Sir/Ma'am", "I request you to", "kindly do the needful", "revert back", "post 11am" for "after 11 a.m.", "prepone". Common in some regions does not excuse them.
6b. Placeholders such as "<NAME>", "XYZ" or "[Your Name]" in place of a real name or detail are convention errors. A real email would not contain them. List each one in "deductions" and "issues" (type "register"). Fix: invent a short real-sounding name.
7. A dropped apostrophe or a there/their slip is minor. A wrong word form ("its become" for "it has become") or a non-word is a language error.
8. No praise inflation. The opinion opens with the score and the main reason it is not higher. Each strength quotes evidence.
9. Be consistent: the same response gets the same score. If you cannot quote evidence for the higher score, give the lower one.
10. Spoken answers judged from a transcript only: you cannot hear pace, pronunciation or intonation. Say so. Do not invent delivery faults or praise.
