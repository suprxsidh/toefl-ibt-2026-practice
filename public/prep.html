Before you start: TOEFL iBT (January 2026 format)

Order: Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking. Total time: about 83 to 89 minutes. Items marked (unofficial) come from prep sites, not ETS.

Test day overview

  • Adaptive means a first set (the router) picks your second set. A good router gives the harder module, which has a higher score ceiling.
  • Reading lets you go back inside a module. You cannot return to Module 1 once Module 2 starts.
  • Listening audio plays once. Questions appear one at a time, each with a countdown (about 20 s for conversations, 30 s for talks). At zero you move on with no answer. You cannot go back.
  • Each section is scored on a band from 1 to 6.

Complete the Words

Many words in a paragraph lose their last letters, like "mi___". Fill them in.

  • Scores well: Read the whole paragraph first. Decide each word from the sentence, then count the letters.
  • Template: "mi___ think th__ prehistoric peo___" becomes "might think that prehistoric people". The grammar tells you "think" needs "that", and "prehistoric" needs a plural noun.
  • Trap 1: Wrong word form. Check -s, -ed, -ing, -tion, -ly against the sentence.
  • Trap 2: Misspelling the full word.

Read in Daily Life

You read a short real-life text, such as a bank notice, then answer 2-3 questions.

  • Scores well: Name the text type. Read the question, then find the line.
  • Example: The notice says "Access your account through the mobile app". So "By using the app" is right, not "the website".
  • Trap 1: Options that sound right but name the wrong channel or person.
  • Trap 2: Reading every word. Scan instead.

Read an Academic Passage

  • Scores well: Read each paragraph's first sentence. Answer from a line you can point to.
  • Trap 1: An option true in real life but not in the passage.
  • Trap 2: Extreme words such as "always" and "never".

Listen and Choose a Response

You hear one line and pick the best of four replies.

  • Scores well: Predict a reply, then match it.
  • Example: "I'm not available tomorrow." The best reply is "What's a better day for you?"
  • Trap 1 (same-sound words): "How can I get this light to work?" The wrong choices "The lighter one is over there" and "I prefer to work at home" reuse "light" and "work".
  • Trap 2 (wrong tense or person): "Did you bring the documents?" The wrong choice "Yes, I would" uses the wrong tense. "Wait, isn't the concert tomorrow night?" The wrong choice "No, I can't wait" uses the sound, not the meaning.
  • Trap 3 (yes/no): "Isn't the store open late?" fits "Yes, it is." Hear the negative question before you pick yes or no.

Conversation, Announcement, Academic Talk

You hear audio once, then answer questions on main idea, detail, purpose, and next step.

  • Scores well: Write 3 to 6 keywords, not sentences. Note attitude and changes of plan.
  • Example notes: "laptop freezes / restart + scan x / repair shop". The last note answers "what next".
  • Trap 1: Choosing the first plan you heard. Plans often change.
  • Trap 2: Writing full sentences. You will miss the next line.
  • Tip (unofficial): Use symbols such as "+" and "x". Always write numbers and "but" words.

Build a Sentence

You get a context line and loose words. Put them in grammatical order.

  • Scores well: Find the verb, then the subject, then place the rest.
  • Question order: question word, auxiliary, subject, main verb. Example: "what / time / does / it / start" becomes "What time does it start?"
  • Indirect question: after "Do you know" or "Can you tell me", use statement order, with no "do". Example: "Do you know how much the tickets will cost?"
  • Trap 1: Using question order inside an indirect question.
  • Trap 2: Forgetting that "whether" or "if" starts the clause.

Write an Email

You read a situation and three bullets. You have 7 minutes. ETS says: write as much as you can, in complete sentences. Aim for 120 to 150 words (unofficial).

PartWhat to writeTime
Greeting"Dear Editor,"10 s
Purpose line"I am writing about ..."30 s
BodyOne short paragraph per bullet, in the order given4 min
CloseThanks, next step, "Sincerely," and your name30 s
CheckBullets, spelling, tone1 min
  • Register: Use "Dear" and full sentences for editors, professors, and managers. Use "Hi" and contractions only for a friendly coworker.
  • Scores well: Cover every bullet in one or two sentences. Ask politely: "Could you please tell me ...".
  • Trap 1: Skipping one bullet. Tick each bullet before you finish.
  • Trap 2: A rude or casual tone when you complain.

Model email (prompt: poetry magazine form problem, 125 words)

Dear Editor,

I am writing about the two poems I sent to your magazine. First, I want to say that I enjoy the new issue. The mix of new and established poets feels fresh, and the short layout is easy to read.

However, I had trouble with the online submission form. After I uploaded my second poem, the page froze and showed no confirmation message. I tried again, and the same thing happened.

Could you please tell me whether you received both poems? If you did not, I can send them by email instead.

Thank you for your time. I look forward to your reply.

Sincerely,
Dana Rivera

Write for an Academic Discussion

You read a professor's question and two classmate posts. You have 10 minutes. ETS says an effective answer has at least 100 words. Aim for 120 to 150 (unofficial).

PartWhat to writeTime

ClassmateName one classmate and agree or disagree2 min

  • Scores well: State your view in your own words. Add something new.
  • Trap 1: Repeating a classmate's point.
  • Trap 2: An opinion with no example.

Model post (prompt: required volunteer hours, 140 words)

I disagree with requiring volunteer hours. Like the second student, I worry about students with little free time, but I want to add a different point. Forced service can reduce the care it hopes to build. When students must collect hours, they often pick the easiest task and treat it as a box to tick. At my school, many classmates signed forms for hours they barely worked.

A better policy is to make volunteering optional and reward it, for example with a note on the diploma. Students who choose a cause they care about stay longer and learn more.

The first student says teenagers do not think about helping others. I agree they need an introduction. Schools could host one voluntary service day each term. That gives the exposure without the pressure.

ETS does not state seconds per response. Plan about 45 seconds per interview answer (unofficial).

Listen and Repeat

You hear a sentence once and repeat it. Early items are short, later items are longer. ETS says: repeat only once.

  • Scores well: Copy words, order, and endings exactly, at a steady pace. Exactness beats speed.
  • Example: chunk "Please, no outside food or drinks / and do not feed the animals."
  • Trap 1: Dropping the last words or changing word endings, such as "drinks" to "drink".
  • Trap 2: Stopping to fix a mistake.

Take an Interview

An interviewer asks 4 questions in a row. You get no preparation time. ETS says: say as much as you can in the time allowed.

StepWhat to sayExample
AnswerDirect answer in the first sentence"I would take public transport."
ReasonOne clear reason"It costs much less."
ExampleA short real example"Last year I saved 40 dollars a month."
WrapOne closing line"The extra time is worth it."
  • Trap 1: Long silence before you start.
  • Trap 2: Stopping early. Add detail until the clock ends.
